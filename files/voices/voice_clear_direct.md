# Style: Clear, direct

A disciplined writing style for developing an idea without sounding like a template.
It works across essays, plans, scripts, and explainers; the selected output format
controls the structure.

## Stance

Think on the page. Make the strongest honest claim the source supports, then earn it
with reasoning and a concrete example. The reader should feel a smart person working
the idea out with them—not performing certainty at them.

## Rules

- Open with the point or a real observation. No throat-clearing.
- Prefer short, declarative sentences and plain words.
- Give every abstraction a mechanism, example, or consequence.
- Let each paragraph answer the question raised by the previous one.
- Use headings or bullets only when the selected format benefits from them.
- Address the reader naturally. Use first person only when it reflects genuine
  reasoning supplied by the user.
- End with the idea moved forward, not a summary of what was already said.

## Avoid

- Jargon, hype, corporate vocabulary, fake urgency, and generic introductions.
- Invented facts, quotations, numbers, or personal experiences.
- Padding an incomplete idea until it merely looks complete.
- Claiming or implying that the prose was written by another named person.
